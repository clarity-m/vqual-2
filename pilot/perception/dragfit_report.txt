  airborne   = LOCAL_POSITION_NED z < -0.5 m. Non-negotiable: a drone parked on
               the 17.8 deg start pad reads a CONSTANT |a_xy| = 3.00 m/s^2 at zero
               speed (pad normal force through a tilted airframe), and it is 34-99%
               of several sessions. Pooled in, it pins the intercept on an artefact.
  noContact  = collision EPISODES (>0.5 s gaps) with a 0.5 s guard either side.
               Raw |a_xy| reaches 106 m/s^2; nothing aerodynamic does that.
  steady     = additionally |gyro| <= 1.5 rad/s, |v| >= 5 m/s, in runs lasting
               >= 0.5 s. This is the "clean fast straight-ish" population.

THE LAW. Horizontal body specific force is quadratic in the IN-PLANE speed
component, not in the total speed:

    a_body[0:2] = -k * |v_xy| * v_xy

  candidate laws, pooled, airborne + no contact, |v| >= 2 m/s (n = 18355):
    R2 0.8259   total          k = [0.02484]
    R2 0.9562   inplane        k = [0.04249]
    R2 0.8840   total+lin      k = [0.25812 0.01249]
    R2 0.9564   inplane+lin    k = [0.01842 0.04101]

  Binning by TOTAL speed makes the implied coefficient saturate above ~14 m/s and
  the law look broken; binning by |v_xy| makes it flat over 2-25 m/s. That is
  anisotropic drag (the airframe has its own coefficient in the rotor plane), and
  it matters twice over: it fits far better (R2 0.956 vs 0.826), and it makes the
  inversion |v_xy| = sqrt(|a_xy|/k) exact -- no assumption inside it at all.

  |v_xy| is what SHIPS: median 0.53 m/s, p90 0.88 m/s, bias +0.21, ~7% relative,
  and flat across every speed band from 2 to 34 m/s.

  |v| is what IS REFUSED: median looks fine at 1.24 m/s but p90 is 14.96 m/s and the
  22-40 m/s band carries a -14.3 m/s bias. The total speed needs the body-z
  velocity component, which is not observable (thrust sits on that axis; the
  barometer reads nan in this sim). Closing the system with a world-horizontal-
  velocity assumption is what fails: the recorded flight-path angle has a 26-34 deg
  median and only a third to two thirds of frames sit under 20 deg.

  So speed_est_mps carries the IN-PLANE speed and says so, and
  (vel_bearing_rad, speed_est_mps) together are the body-horizontal velocity
  vector. Nothing in the stack claims to know the climb rate.
